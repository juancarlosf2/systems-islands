# Interaction roadmap

**Status: the first interaction layer is implemented across all sixteen worlds in v1.1.0.** Model-instrumented jobs, contextual changes using existing controls, reserved site paths, Play beneath the render, pause/step/resume, job following and completed-trace browsing are implemented. See [the implementation contract](PLAYABLE_INTERACTIONS.md) and [validation](VALIDATION.md). New staffing costs, custom incident commands, seeded challenge modes and additional architecture policies remain planned. The detailed proposals below describe the broader destination; they are not a claim that every phase has shipped. Existing mission objectives, configuration snapshots, scoring and earned-star semantics are preserved.

The goal is to make a visitor, operator, courier or technician visibly perform a chapter-specific job, and let the player understand or change that job through a small contextual interaction. Walking should explain the system: a courier waits because admission is closed, a technician repairs a stale replica because a reconciliation job exists, and a receipt returns only after the modeled acceptance condition. Decorative activity must never imply successful processing that the simulation did not perform.

## Current behavior and preserved boundaries

Outside trials, crews retain their entrance/workstation/exit idle loop. During trials, sampled visitors, operators, couriers and technicians follow actual chapter operation traces, reserve distinct site or queue positions, carry role-colored props and use task-specific poses. Configuration forecasts and deterministic results influence staffing and hazards. Terrain and conservative footprint checks gate site-to-site paths; this remains a finite navigation system rather than a general physics engine.

The trial presentation lasts six seconds. The mission model remains a pure, deterministic educational calculation from an immutable configuration/scenario snapshot. Domain time is distinct from presentation time: chapter 7 uses milliseconds, chapter 9 permits a ten-second crawl, and chapter 2 estimates yearly downtime. Compressing or pausing the six-second presentation must not change those model results.

### Implemented performance foundation

The chart batches static environment geometry while preserving all **16 islands, 32 chart crew members and 180 chart paths**. Chart static mesh count changed from **3,173 to 213**. Tests verify that transformed geometry triangle counts and bounds are unchanged. Level environment meshes remain individually mutable; the level population is capped at **10 workers**. The measurements below were taken for the v1.0.1 rendering foundation before the interaction implementation.

Before/after captures used the same Chrome/T3 environment, a **1280 × 800** viewport, device DPR **2**, renderer DPR capped at **1.7**, and an **eight-second** sampling window per capture:

| Measurement | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chart median WebGL draw calls | 3,880 | 920 |
| Chart median render-callback duration | 9.9 ms | 2.4 ms |
| Chart p95 render-callback duration | 11.1 ms | 3.4 ms |
| Chapter 1 median render-callback duration | 2.1 ms | 2.1 ms |
| Chapter 1 median WebGL draw calls | 839 | 839 |

The intentional approximately **30 fps cap** is unchanged; median frame gap remains **33.3 ms**. Render-callback duration is elapsed time around the JavaScript/Three.js callback, including WebGL submission calls. It is not a CPU-only profile, GPU execution time or total frame cost. These captures are not physical-phone benchmarks and do not establish a 60 fps target or compatibility across devices.

Foundation validation recorded **55 passing tests**, crew validation across **16 chapters × 21 damage phases with zero collisions**, asset validation and a successful portable build. These checks support the geometry/navigation/build foundation; they do not prove the future interaction lifecycle, new strategy policies or phone performance. Later pilot measurements should use a comparable procedure and retain their own device and coverage limits.

Preserve these invariants:

- Existing three-objective win checks and star/completion rules remain authoritative. An animation, path arrival, crew count or player camera gesture cannot award a win or prevent an otherwise valid result.
- Inputs remain immediate before a trial. A running trial uses its captured configuration; editing a later configuration cannot retroactively alter the captured result. Leaving a chapter cancels active presentation and discards its transient reservations and callbacks.
- Keep the pure simulation layer, React controls, Three.js world, existing navigation metadata and Blender-authored art. Introduce no backend, remote asset requirement or dependency as part of this proposal. Preserve the portable edition's offline behavior.
- Retain the current articulated worker and scale: approximately **0.3784 scene units tall**, with **0.46-unit-high** building openings. Props and new poses must fit the opening and collision envelope. Preserve distinct entrance and exit routes and authored bridge access.
- Preserve the minimal retro UI, accessible HTML controls, keyboard equivalents, touch support, game and operating-system reduced-motion settings, honest WebGL fallback, and all sixteen freely accessible chapters.
- Staffing is visual in the first phase. A favorable result can restore a visual crew; that crew must not then feed back into capacity and make the same result pass. Later staffing mechanics require explicit costs and capacity equations derived from configuration and actions before evaluation.

The older game plan permits dramatic damage following severe failures. This proposal refines future consequences: a budget breach or a missed latency target causes a corresponding staffing shortage, queue or service warning; neither automatically collapses a building. Structural effects remain reserved for an explicitly modeled physical incident and never substitute for evidence of data loss.

## NPC jobs, sites and safe movement

### Roles and lifecycle

Reuse the worker body and articulated pivots. Role identity comes from a small Blender-authored accessory, carried object and task pose, with textual identity available through inspection:

| Role | Responsibility | Examples of semantic work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Visitor | Generates or consumes a modeled request | Join a fair queue, read a feed, receive a message, watch a segment |
| Operator | Performs admitted site work | Consume a token, record a version, rank suggestions, publish a manifest |
| Courier | Moves a represented job or result between sites | Carry an accepted queue job, migrate a changed key, deliver a durable receipt |
| Technician | Executes a modeled repair or recovery operation | Reconcile a replica, restore an encoder, resume a completed-block cursor |

The normal lifecycle is `arrive → queue → reserve workstation → walk → perform task → await acknowledgment → handoff or exit`. Explicit branches are `admission rejected → exit`, `retry scheduled → waiting slot`, and `site unavailable → safe refuge or reroute`. A task may generate a rejection or timeout rather than an acknowledgment. A valid read does not need a fictitious durable-write step; each chapter supplies its own acceptance prerequisite.

Reservation is a visual occupancy claim, separate from authoritative service capacity. A site has authored work slots, waiting slots and safe departure points. Claim a unique slot before starting a route, hold it until departure clears the workstation, and release it on cancellation or retirement. A FIFO visual waiting list can represent many jobs; fair model scheduling determines which job is serviced. Do not infer model fairness from the apparent order of NPC bodies.

One NPC represents a bounded aggregate, never every resident, follower or request. For example, a chapter 11 visitor may stand for a reader cohort; a visible courier may carry a batch of fanout jobs. Inspection exposes the aggregate count and job identities sampled from that cohort. Aggregate completions must reconcile to actual trace counts; a successful-looking courier cannot represent a mixture of dropped and delivered work without explicitly showing that distinction. Keep identities stable within a replay so a followed sample remains the same sample.

### Navigation and workforce changes

Build on the existing terrain polygon, conservative building footprints, deck heights, access points and bridge connectivity. Add slot metadata to existing `landmark_<key>` groups; do not reconstruct island geometry or infer walkable shortcuts from straight lines.

Cache validated site-to-site paths by chapter, navigation revision, source slot, destination slot and worker clearance. Use the existing path validator on every newly computed route. A topology change invalidates affected cached paths. Slot occupancy changes which destination is available; it does not make a route through another building safe. A logical partition can disable communication while a bridge remains physically walkable; block the walking route only when the authored incident actually makes that bridge unsafe.

When no route exists, remain at a known safe slot, show the blocked handoff in inspection, and release a destination reservation that cannot be reached. Never teleport through a hazard, hide an NPC while it is stranded, or claim a successful delivery because a fallback position was found. Unconnected sites must be resolved in authored navigation before their physical handoff is enabled.

On staffing reduction, stop allocating new work to retiring visual workers. Let their current visual task reach a safe interruption point, release reservations, then use a validated evacuation route to the existing exit. If the exit is temporarily inaccessible, move to an authored safe refuge and retain the worker visibly until a route is restored. The authoritative job remains queued, completes or fails according to the model; a disappearing avatar must not silently erase accepted work. Replacement crews enter through the entrance after a valid arrival slot is available. Budget shortages reduce modeled staff only in the later staffing phase, rather than magically destroying structures.

Reduced motion renders role, occupancy and current task as static states. It suppresses walking, shaking and task gestures while retaining event order, acknowledgment state, reservation exclusivity and the same trial result. Turning reduced motion on mid-route moves presentation to a validated safe slot and releases or rebinds the old visual reservation without altering job state.

## Player interaction priorities

Keep one compact context surface for the currently selected site. Show **one to three actions** applicable to that site's state, using the same retro buttons and native focus behavior. Do not add permanent labels over every worker, a second settings dashboard or an always-open event log. Full detail stays in inspection, the field guide or the debrief.

| Priority | Player option | Concrete behavior and boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P0 | Select a site | Click/tap the landmark or use its existing HTML landmark button. Open inspection with up to three actions such as “Follow one job,” “Inspect waiting work,” or “Stage capacity.” Escape or close returns focus to the initiating control. |
| P0 | Inspect/follow one packet | Select a stable sampled job, show its current site, version, attempt and last event, and optionally track its courier. Following is reversible; it does not steer routing or change results. |
| P0 | Pause/single-step a trace | Pause presentation and advance one causally ordered event. Resume without repeating side effects or changing simulation time. Keep the ordinary six-second run as the default. |
| P1 | Assign a crew | Before a trial, allocate a role to a site through existing capacity controls where possible. In the visual phase this changes representation only; clearly disclose that fact in inspection. Actual workforce capacity appears only with the explicit staffing model. |
| P1 | Route couriers | Choose between modeled eligible next hops, not arbitrary walking waypoints. Pretrial routing becomes part of the immutable configuration. A new route option requires a simulation policy and tests, not merely a prettier path. |
| P1 | Stage capacity | Preview a server, consumer or replica addition and its cost. Applying it updates supported pretrial configuration; it cannot resize the captured trial halfway through playback. |
| P1 | Intervene in an incident | Initially show existing modeled recovery choices. Later challenge modes can accept bounded, time-stamped commands such as isolate, retry or reroute; replay records the command and its cost. No unlogged mouse timing can affect a win. |
| P2 | Make a pretrial prediction | Select the likely bottleneck or expected trade-off before running. Compare with observed metrics in the debrief. Prediction never blocks a chapter or replaces its win predicates. |
| P2 | Seeded challenges and replay | Choose a declared seed and workload mode, capture config plus any intervention log, and replay the same event order. Default missions retain their current workload assumptions. |

Keyboard actions must work without raycasting or camera movement. Avoid adding global shortcuts that conflict with `R`, `G`, `C`, typing or dialogs. Touch uses tap and discrete controls; no essential action requires hover, dragging a small NPC or precise double taps. Camera following can be disabled and must yield to deliberate orbit/pan. Event stepping stays accessible through HTML when WebGL is unavailable.

## Educational rules to add deliberately

1. **Capacity, admission and fairness.** A site has model service capacity and a finite or explicitly bounded queue. Distinguish rejected, queued, timed-out and completed work. Track per-client admission and wait when fairness matters; total throughput alone cannot conceal starvation. Visual slot limits are not that capacity.
2. **Durable acceptance and acknowledgment order.** For accepted writes/jobs, record the declared durability event before the acceptance acknowledgment. Display an early receipt as a protocol defect, not a success gesture. Separate “upload durably queued” from “all renditions ready”; notification provider success and queue acceptance are different events.
3. **Retry budgets, backoff and idempotency.** Count the initial attempt separately from retries, include scheduled retry time and terminal exhaustion, and distinguish repeated attempts from duplicate side effects. A bounded idempotency model must not claim universal exactly-once delivery. Over-retrying consumes the mission's attempt/time budget.
4. **Version and freshness.** Attach comparable versions, logical sequence or snapshot age to relevant events. A visually busy cache can still serve stale state. Repairs must select the version under the chapter's declared ordering policy; wall-clock animation time is not a version.
5. **Cost and staffing.** Preserve existing coin checks. Later workforce mechanics specify staff cost, effective service rate and redeployment delay before a run. A worker count is an input-derived resource or an output visualization, never both through a circular feedback loop. Do not penalize a chapter with an invented staffing budget it does not currently have.
6. **Causal maintenance.** Add a repair job only when a modeled fault and repair operation exist. Maintenance consumes time/capacity only when those costs are modeled. Inspecting a site or playing a wrench gesture does not heal it.
7. **Recovery and loss.** Separate temporary delay, safe rejection, retained backlog, breached recovery deadline and genuine loss of accepted state. A grace period can postpone visual abandonment, but cannot forgive a violated correctness objective. An isolated vault rejecting writes safely is different from acknowledging and losing them.
8. **Multiple valid strategies.** Evaluate outcome predicates and measured constraints, not equality with `level.solution`. Show throughput, cost, delay, freshness or availability trade-offs only where modeled. A candidate is Pareto-preferred only if it improves a modeled dimension without worsening another; do not invent a single universal best architecture.

Changing a workload, introducing a new policy or adding a staffing model creates a separately identified challenge version. It needs explicit simulation support and tests before rollout. It must not quietly relax baseline objectives or reuse an old earned star as evidence of passing the new challenge.

## Proposed architecture contracts

### Pure evaluation and trace

Keep `evaluateLevel(id, config, options)` and its existing result fields compatible. Existing summary `events` contain `{type, message}`; they cannot be assumed to contain timed per-job data. Add a versioned trace alongside that summary only when concrete chapter operations can emit it. Do not synthesize successful task histories from a pass/fail flag or invent a random trace to fill the island.

The proposed input record is `{chapterId, scenarioVersion, seed, configSnapshot, actionLog}`. Ordinary trials have an empty action log and the existing deterministic scenario. A future interactive challenge evaluates a finite, validated command log in the pure layer; it does not read wall-clock time, renderer state or NPC positions. Unknown commands, illegal target sites, negative costs and commands outside the scenario window are rejected explicitly. No persistence of large traces is required: store a small replay descriptor and regenerate the trace locally.

Example payload concepts, to be finalized against the pilot's concrete operations:

```js
{
  traceVersion: 1,
  chapterId: 10,
  scenarioVersion: 'notification-baseline-v1',
  seed: 17,
  events: [{
    sequence: 12,
    domainTimeMs: 500,
    kind: 'attempt.started',
    jobId: 'permitted-3',
    aggregateCount: 1,
    siteKey: 'channels',
    fromSiteKey: 'queue',
    attempt: 2,
    causes: [8, 11]
  }]
}
```

The example is a proposed shape, not a captured run. Optional chapter-specific fields include client/recipient identity, version, durability scope, idempotency key, queue depth, admission decision, outcome, bytes, cost and fault ID. Include only fields with an actual model meaning. Keep BigInt IDs serializable as decimal strings. Event IDs and tie-break order are stable for the same replay descriptor; causal references point backward to known events. Do not add generic protocol stages that every chapter must fake.

Preserve exact domain timestamp ordering for rate-limit boundaries, rollback stamps and retry deadlines. Existing analytic/formula models need no artificial timestep conversion. If a future dynamic model concretely needs a fixed simulation timestep, choose and test it for that model; a provisional 20 ms tick is a profiling candidate, not a repository-wide scheduling requirement or an implemented change. Process due events at their exact domain timestamp rather than rounding millisecond workloads into buckets. Presentation interpolation runs at renderer cadence; seeded sampling chooses which aggregates to show and does not enter the evaluator's win calculation.

### Event-to-animation mapping

| Modeled event | Presentation | Forbidden implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Request arrived or frontier item discovered | Visitor/parcel enters or joins a waiting slot | Arrival does not prove admission. |
| Work admitted/dequeued | Operator claims a task and courier begins a validated handoff | A free visual slot does not create model capacity. |
| Durable acceptance recorded | Parcel secured; receipt becomes eligible | A hand gesture before persistence cannot imply an ACK. |
| Read/write/encode/rank work performed | Role-specific bounded task pose | Pose completion cannot generate a model completion. |
| Attempt failed or quota rejected | Return to wait, safe rejection or exit | Not every failure destroys work or structures. |
| Retry deadline reached | Waiting job returns to dispatch | A looping courier cannot add attempts. |
| Version repaired or chunk resumed | Technician reconciles the actual version/cursor | Repair animation cannot change correctness. |
| Acknowledgment or successful side effect emitted | Return receipt or deliver result | Receiving an ACK is not universal final readiness. |

Every visible semantic task stores the event IDs it represents. Inspection explains those events; validation can assert that no successful receipt exists without its required predecessor. Neutral idle animations may convey waiting, but carry no completion, repair or success meaning. If the visual scheduler cannot keep up, compress aggregate movement and preserve ordered state transitions; never delay, drop or fabricate authoritative events to fit six seconds.

### World and input boundary

Reuse the current `World` props and inspection callback. Add trace playback and contextual actions through the smallest necessary compatible interface after the pilot makes the data concrete. The world receives evaluated state and emits player intent; it does not own acceptance, cost or scoring. A separate presentation record can hold actor ID, role, current slot, reserved slot, route revision, task event IDs and lifecycle state. This is an implementation requirement, not a mandate to add a generalized entity framework.

Each trial/navigation revision owns its callbacks and reservations. Stale callbacks after chapter switch, replay restart or config replacement are ignored. Limit path lookup attempts, cached routes and actor population. A canceled task releases its visual claim exactly once. The semantic DOM surface and world selection refer to the same existing landmark key.

## Meaningful validation and provisional budgets

Future implementation must run the repository's established `npm test`, `npm run validate:assets`, `npm run validate:crew`, and `npm run build` checks, plus focused behavior tests justified by the implemented phase. Documentation alone does not establish those results.

Required behavior evidence:

- **Determinism and conservation:** identical seed, snapshot and action log yield identical event ordering, metrics and objectives; evaluating and replaying do not mutate inputs. Accepted jobs reconcile to completed, still retained or explicitly lost jobs. Duplicate events/attempts cannot create extra side effects. Pause/step and actor-count changes leave evaluation unchanged.
- **Protocol boundaries:** chapter 4 checks adversarial boundary timestamps, both gateway identities and per-client admission. Chapter 10 checks retry exhaustion, preference check before provider call, outage recovery and ACK ordering. Chapters 6/12/15/16 distinguish safe rejection/retention from loss of acknowledged or accepted state.
- **Multi-strategy acceptance:** test outcome predicates with at least two structurally distinct passing strategies when each extension lands, plus focused failure cases for their trade-offs. Existing `solution` remains a fixture rather than an answer-equality check. Test baseline and changed-workload challenges separately.
- **Navigation/occupancy:** reachable site pairs, corridor/door clearance, unique reservation ownership, site removal, workforce reduction during work, blocked exit/refuge recovery, canceled routes and chapter switches. A geometry validator plus sampled live paths is needed; a screenshot cannot prove safe traversal.
- **UI/accessibility:** select each pilot site by keyboard and touch, execute contextual actions, follow/cancel follow, pause/step/resume, close with focus restored, switch chapter while running, and toggle reduced motion mid-task. Verify GLB loading, orbit/pinch versus tap selection, no hover dependency, background/foreground playback behavior and an offline `file://` run. Preserve honest coverage limits.
